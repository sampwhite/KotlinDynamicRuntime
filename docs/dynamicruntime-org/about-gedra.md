# About Gedra

Gedra runs applications built around forms. People fill in forms, others review and approve them, and the results end
up in reports: a call for talks, a grant program, a vendor's onboarding. Each application's forms, its steps, the
people who take part, and its wording are configuration rather than code. Its administrators change them while it
runs, and nothing has to be written or deployed for a change to take effect.

This site, dynamicruntime.org, runs Gedra so you can try it. Its data is kept in a temporary database that is cleared
every month, so nothing you add here lasts.

## Where the name comes from

Gedra began as KDR, short for KotlinDynamicRuntime, the open-source engine it is built on. Give KDR vowels and you get
"Kedra"; swap the K for the G of Gyassa Software, which makes it, and you get Gedra, said with a hard G.

The word is also the name of the one kind of record the platform stores. A form is a gedra, and so is what it keeps
about a user, a workflow, or a file. Every application built on it is made of gedras, which is why the product took the
name.

## Who makes it

Gedra is made by Gyassa Software ([gyassa.com](https://gyassa.com)), a Minnesota company.

It is built on [KotlinDynamicRuntime](https://github.com/sampwhite/KotlinDynamicRuntime), which is open source under
the [MIT License](https://github.com/sampwhite/KotlinDynamicRuntime/blob/main/LICENSE). Its repository has the code,
the documentation, and a guide to running your own copy.
